Project 02 · AI-directed software delivery

BoomArena

From a local bomb-battle prototype to a real-time multiplayer product.

Play live demo ↗

The question

Could product judgment and architectural direction become working software without manually writing the code? I explored that question through a browser game for two to five friends.

My role and AI credit

I conceived and directed BoomArena, including its product vision, scope, architecture decisions, requirements, acceptance criteria, validation and releases. ChatGPT/Codex generated and modified the implementation code under my direction; I did not manually author the codebase.

How it evolved

The project began with deterministic two-player local gameplay. I expanded the match loop before tackling online distribution. The major transition was extracting a renderer-independent shared simulation and introducing private WebSocket rooms with a server-authoritative Cloudflare Durable Object for each room. Later milestones refined mobile controls, reconnection and state synchronization, then added sudden death and team modes.

Architecture at a glance

Browser: Phaser rendering and keyboard/touch inputs
Transport: WebSockets through Cloudflare Worker routing
Coordination: One GameRoom Durable Object per private room
Rules: Phaser-free shared TypeScript simulation, authoritative at 20 Hz
Updates: Versioned protocol v4 with baseline and compact snapshots at 10 Hz
Client: Interpolation, prediction and reconciliation

Decision: keep the server authoritative

Players must agree on movement, bombs, damage, scoring and results. Server authority creates a single source of truth, at the cost of more complicated client responsiveness and reconciliation.

Decision: share the simulation

Local and online modes use the same core gameplay rules rather than maintaining two implementations. That improves consistency and testability while requiring gameplay logic to remain serializable and independent of Phaser.

Decision: protect the MVP

Invite-only guest rooms support the intended social experience without accounts or public matchmaking. Fixed reactions provide social presence without free-text chat. These boundaries kept the first complete product focused.

Validation and outcome

The repository review dated September 19, 2026 reported 13 milestone commits and 136 passing tests (124 core, 12 Worker integration), along with successful TypeScript type checking and ESLint. These are build-quality observations, not user-scale or commercial-impact claims. Recheck before publishing updated figures.

What I learned

AI-generated implementation still needs human judgment about scope, architecture and acceptance. A technically functioning feature also needs real usability validation; mobile controls and networking required targeted refinement after their initial releases.
