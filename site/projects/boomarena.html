01 · Project overview

From local gameplay to private online rooms

BoomArena is an independent experiment in directing AI-assisted product delivery through architectural decisions, clear requirements, iterative development, and validation.

The project evolved from a deterministic two-player local browser game into a real-time multiplayer application supporting private online rooms for two to five friends.

AI implementation disclosure

I owned the product vision, architectural direction, requirements, validation, and release decisions. AI coding agents generated and modified the implementation under my direction.

02 · The problem / motivation

Can product and architecture direction become a working real-time system?

The starting question was whether product judgment and architectural direction could become working software without manually writing the implementation. A browser game for a small group of friends created a useful test: it needed clear rules, responsive interaction, synchronized state, and deliberately constrained scope.

The difficult transition was not simply putting the local game online. Every player needed to agree on movement, bombs, damage, scoring, and match results while the experience still felt responsive across desktop and mobile browsers.

03 · My role

Directing scope, architecture, milestones, and validation

I conceived and directed BoomArena, including its product vision, scope, architectural decisions, requirements, acceptance criteria, validation, and releases.

My focus was on framing the next useful increment, evaluating tradeoffs, reviewing behavior, and deciding when a result was ready to move forward. The implementation was generated and modified by AI coding agents under that direction.

04 · Product vision and scope

A focused social game, not a public gaming platform

The project protected a narrow use case: quick matches among invited friends. Later milestones expanded the play experience without introducing public-platform infrastructure.

Private multiplayer

Invite-only rooms support online games for two to five friends.

Shared game rules

Local and online modes use the same renderer-independent simulation.

Desktop and mobile

Keyboard and touch inputs feed the same game model, with mobile controls refined through iteration.

Bounded social features

Guest rooms and fixed reactions provide presence without accounts, matchmaking, or free-text chat.

05 · Architecture

A shared simulation with server authority

The browser handles Phaser rendering and keyboard or touch input. WebSockets connect through a Cloudflare Worker to one GameRoom Durable Object per private room. The Durable Object runs the Phaser-free shared TypeScript simulation as the authoritative source of game state.

06 · Key architectural decisions

Architecture that evolved with the game

The online design followed a working local match loop. Simulation extraction, server authority, synchronization, and reconnect behavior were introduced and refined through later milestones.

DECISION 01

Shared deterministic simulation independent of Phaser

Problem

Local and online play needed consistent rules without maintaining separate gameplay implementations.

Decision

Extract a shared, Phaser-free TypeScript simulation for the core game rules.

Rationale

Both modes can use the same deterministic behavior, improving consistency and testability.

Tradeoff

Gameplay state must remain serializable and independent of renderer-specific objects.

Learning

Separating rules from presentation creates a stronger foundation for both alternate clients and authoritative execution.

DECISION 02

Server-authoritative multiplayer

Problem

Players needed one agreed state for movement, bombs, damage, scoring, and results.

Decision

Make the server-side room simulation the source of truth instead of trusting client state.

Rationale

Server authority gives every connected client a consistent basis for match outcomes.

Tradeoff

Client responsiveness becomes more complex, requiring interpolation, prediction, and reconciliation.

Learning

Consistency and responsiveness are connected design problems; authority alone does not create a good player experience.

DECISION 03

One Durable Object per private room

Problem

Each private match needed an isolated coordinator for connections and authoritative state.

Decision

Map every private game room to its own Cloudflare GameRoom Durable Object.

Rationale

The room is the natural boundary for connected players, match state, and real-time coordination.

Tradeoff

The design introduces explicit Worker routing and room lifecycle responsibilities.

Learning

A stateful infrastructure primitive is most useful when its ownership boundary closely matches the product concept.

DECISION 04

Versioned WebSocket protocol with baselines and compact snapshots

Problem

Clients needed initial and reconnect state as well as efficient recurring updates while the protocol evolved.

Decision

Use a versioned protocol—currently described as v4—with baseline state and compact snapshots sent at 10 Hz.

Rationale

Baselines establish full state; compact snapshots reduce the recurring update shape between 20 Hz simulation ticks.

Tradeoff

Protocol versions and snapshot formats add coordination and compatibility complexity.

Learning

A real-time protocol benefits from explicit evolution and distinct paths for initial state and frequent updates.

DECISION 05

Invite-only guest rooms

Problem

The intended experience was playing with friends, not operating a public player network.

Decision

Use invite-only guest rooms rather than public matchmaking and account infrastructure.

Rationale

The boundary supports the core social experience while protecting the MVP from unrelated platform scope.

Tradeoff

There is no persistent account identity, player discovery, or public matchmaking.

Learning

Excluding infrastructure can be an architectural decision when it keeps the product aligned with its actual use case.

07 · Challenges and iterations

The multiplayer system followed a proven local loop

The project began with deterministic two-player local gameplay. I expanded the match loop before tackling online distribution, then extracted the renderer-independent simulation and introduced private WebSocket rooms with server authority.

Later milestones refined mobile controls, reconnection, and state synchronization before adding sudden death and team modes. The sequence matters: the final architecture was reached through iteration, not specified in full at the beginning.

08 · Validation

Automated checks and targeted product refinement

The existing repository review dated September 19, 2026 reports 13 milestone commits and 136 passing tests: 124 core tests and 12 Worker integration tests. It also reports successful TypeScript type checking and ESLint.

Usability and networking were also refined after initial implementation, particularly mobile controls, reconnect behavior, and synchronization.

These are build-quality observations, not user-scale or commercial-impact claims. The figures should be rechecked against the BoomArena repository before future publication updates.

10 · Lessons learned

Technical completion and product quality are different thresholds

AI-generated implementation still needs human judgment about scope, architecture, and acceptance. A technically functioning feature also needs real usability validation; mobile controls and networking required targeted refinement after their initial releases.

The project also reinforced the value of sequencing architectural risk. Establishing the match loop first made it possible to focus later work on distribution, authority, and synchronization without simultaneously redefining the game.

11 · What I would explore next

Deepen resilience without losing the focused scope

The next useful exploration would be broader real-device and network-condition validation, with additional attention to reconnection edge cases, protocol compatibility, and the boundary between prediction and reconciliation.

That work would strengthen the existing private-room experience. Public matchmaking and account infrastructure would remain separate product decisions rather than automatic next steps.
